Before excavation

Pool permits and site preparation in the Pittsburgh area

Good pool projects begin before the excavator arrives. Property layout, local requirements, utilities, drainage and construction access should be understood first.

Local rules are local

Western Pennsylvania is made up of many municipalities, and requirements can differ. Setbacks, fencing, electrical inspections and other permit details should be confirmed for the specific property.

Locate utilities and plan equipment

Gas, electric, sewer, septic, water and communication lines can affect pool placement. Equipment should also be positioned for service access, reasonable plumbing runs and noise considerations.

Construction access matters

Excavators, loaders, material deliveries and—on fiberglass projects—the pool shell itself need a workable path into the yard. Gates, side-yard width, slopes, overhead lines and retaining walls should be considered early.

Plan drainage, not just the pool

Pittsburgh-area terrain can move a lot of water. Grading and drainage around the finished pool should direct water appropriately rather than toward the pool, house or neighboring property.

Think about the finished backyard

Pool placement should make room for usable deck space, fencing, furniture, landscaping and the way people will actually move around the yard.

Note: This is general planning information, not legal, engineering or municipal-code advice. Requirements should be confirmed for your property.
